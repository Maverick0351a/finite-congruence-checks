02 / The local reason

One reply cannot block every route.

Start from a certificate card

A card lists cells Maker must already own, an envelope free of Breaker cells, and a bound on the additional Maker claims needed.

Make a fresh claim

Maker claims the card's pivot. If Maker already owns it, a fresh replacement still costs one move. The finite-board argument ensures such a square is available.

Keep a surviving continuation

Every cell shared by all child envelopes is already owned by Maker. Breaker must claim a fresh cell, so its reply cannot spoil every child. Maker follows one that remains available.

TWO DIFFERENT COUNTS

The chosen child may have a much smaller bound. That does not mean Maker played several moves at once. The viewer keeps actual claims, the 21-claim allowance, and each card's remaining bound separate.

03 / Follow the exact evidence

What supports this explanation?

The browser displays saved states; it does not run Lean. A separate checker replays this particular execution. The Lean theorem establishes the strategy guarantee under its formal game rules. The displayed execution is not a formally verified extraction of that strategy.

The paper already supplies the finite-board result. This project checks the certificate and formalizes the finite-board guarantee. The example does not establish a better move bound, optimality, or improved AI reasoning.
