01 / Follow the rules

Together in. Together out.

Carrier: {0, 1, 2, 3}

A grouping works when any two objects in the same group still land in the same group after each rule. The output group may differ from the input group.

f swaps the objects in each pair. g sends the second pair onto the first.

02 / Account for every possibility

Why exactly these five?

Each listed grouping survives both rules. For example, in {0, 2} | {1, 3}, f swaps the two groups and g sends each group into itself. Inputs that were together remain together.

The order is ⊥ < a < b < ⊤ and ⊥ < c < ⊤. Neither a nor b is comparable to c.

Read the exact Lean classification ↗
1

Merge 0 with 3, or 1 with 2

f forces the other pair. Then g forces 0 with 1. Together these identifications connect all four objects, so only the one-group partition survives.

2

Otherwise, merge 0 with 2, or 1 with 3

f forces both pairs, giving {0, 2} | {1, 3}. Any further merge combines its two blocks into one.

3

Otherwise, keep the two halves separate

Only the pairs {0, 1} and {2, 3} can merge. g forces a merge of 0 with 1 whenever 2 and 3 merge. That leaves exactly ⊥, a, and b.

COMPLETENESS

We have both directions: all five displayed groupings work, and every possible valid grouping is one of them. That is what “the full congruence lattice” requires.

How the formal proof covers every relation

finite_classification checks the 64 symmetric reflexive Boolean relations on four points. Many are not transitive. relation_complete proves that every equivalence relation has such an encoding; all_relations lifts the classification to proposition-valued relations. 03 / Place the example in the argument

Which patterns can rules produce?

Family 206 asks which finite lattices can occur as all the congruences of a nonempty finite algebra. Finding a few valid groupings is not enough: there must be no extra ones.

The original paper defines this question in §1.1, page 4. Its Theorem 2.2, pages 7–8, gives a colored-graph criterion for representation. Open the pinned paper ↗

Turn groupings into colors

For two objects x and y, d(x,y) is the smallest valid grouping that puts them together. It is a lattice label, not a number measuring distance.

For instance, d(2,3) = b: any valid grouping that merges 2 and 3 must also merge 0 and 1. For a lattice label ℓ, recover its grouping by the rule x ~ y exactly when d(x,y) ≤ ℓ.

This is the threshold relation θℓ. At threshold b, the two groups are {0, 1} and {2, 3}; at threshold c, they are {0, 2} and {1, 3}.

What the graph criterion adds

Colors must be symmetric, equal to bottom exactly on the diagonal, obey a join-valued triangle inequality, and distinguish lattice order.

The remaining condition is about paths: choose any nonempty set of seed edges. Apply all maps that can only decrease colors. Every pair whose color is below the join of the seed colors must be connected by the resulting marked edges.

That condition makes the recovered grouping list complete. In the reverse construction the theorem uses all color-decreasing maps as unary operations. Our saved example has 12 such maps, and the two displayed rules already give exactly its five groupings.

The exact Lean statement and its assumptions
Fintype Lat supplies finiteness; Lattice Lat supplies meets and joins; OrderBot Lat supplies a least element. Representable requires a positive carrier size and an order isomorphism onto every congruence of a finite list of total finite-arity operations. An empty operation list is permitted.
The local N5 bridge proves this for the saved two-operation algebra. The general theorem relates representability and existence of a graph witness; it does not supply a stopping rule for a search that has found no witness.
