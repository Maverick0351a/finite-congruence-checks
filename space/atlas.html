THE IDEA

Our tool runs this story in reverse: choose a mathematical shape, then find objects and rules whose complete set of valid groupings makes that shape.

Now give those ideas their mathematical names
Carrier
The objects the rules act on. Here, A, B, and C.
Unary operation
A rule taking one object as input and returning one object.
Partition
A division of all the objects into separate, nonempty groups.
Congruence
A partition that respects every rule: inputs together stay together after each rule.
Congruence lattice
The complete pattern of valid groupings, ordered from finer to coarser.
B2
The name of the four-node square above. Its two middle groupings are incomparable.

Any two nodes of a lattice have a greatest common refinement (their meet) and a least common coarsening (their join). Here, the two middle nodes meet at the bottom and join at the top.

This example relabels the saved B2 construction: A = 2, B = 0, C = 1. The saved operation is (0, 0, 2); the friendly-label operation is (A, B, B).

03 / A useful surprise
Same shape. Different work.
Both systems below use four objects and make a four-level chain, called C4. Their groupings differ. One family needs one rule; the other needs two.
WHY IT MATTERS
Choosing the representation changes the cost. Finding a shape is one task. Finding a simpler way to build that shape is another question our tool can investigate.
“Needs” here means the fewest unary operations for that particular grouping family on that particular carrier. Four objects are necessary for a four-level chain: each strict step must merge at least two groups.
