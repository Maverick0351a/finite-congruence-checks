# Research context for bounded finite graph searches

Prepared October 18, 2026. This note distinguishes a reproducible finite computation from a claim about the general finite lattice representation problem. The experiment uses the definitions in the pinned [FiniteCongruenceGraph comparator](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/lean/ComparatorChallenges/FiniteCongruenceGraph.lean). That comparator contains `sorry` for `graph_criterion`; it supplies the statement being tested, and is not itself a checked proof of the equivalence.

For a finite lattice L and a carrier V with n vertices, a candidate gives one lattice color to every ordered vertex pair. The specified conditions require symmetry, bottom exactly on the diagonal, a join-valued triangle inequality, separation of lattice order, and a connectivity condition for every nonempty set of seed edges. An admissible map is any function from V to V that weakly decreases all colors. The connectivity condition uses images of seeds under every admissible map and then equivalence closure. These are finite conditions for fixed L and n; the implementation still needs independent validation of its enumerations and optimizations.

There is substantial prior work behind this approach. Kenney's 2009 presentation defines maps of lattice-labelled graphs by decreasing edge labels, labels canonical complete graphs by principal congruences, and reconstructs algebras using unary graph endomorphisms. Pages 51–58 are particularly close to this experiment. Consequently, our use of colors, maps, and paths is an implementation of established ideas, with no novelty claim for that framework. [Kenney, *Graphical Algebras—A New Approach to Congruence Lattices*](https://im.saske.sk/ssaos2009/talks/wednesday/Kenney.pdf).

An independent finite check is available through unary operations. For a set E of equivalence relations on V, let End(E) be all functions preserving every relation in E, and let Con(V,F) be all equivalence relations preserved by every function in F. The condition E = Con(V,End(E)) characterizes the concrete congruence lattices on V. The paper also explains why considering unary functions suffices for congruence lattices of arbitrary algebras on a fixed finite carrier. [Jakubíková-Studenovská, Pöschel and Radeleczki, 2017, §§2.1–2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1612.07648v2).

For this experiment, form the threshold relations theta_a = {(x,y): color(x,y) <= a}. Enumerating all partitions of V and retaining exactly those invariant under all admissible maps gives the full congruence lattice of an explicit unary algebra. Comparing that collection, including its inclusion order, with the threshold relations provides a different check from enumerating seed sets. On four vertices there are only 15 partitions, although the number of functions and color assignments grows quickly with n. A successful direct comparison establishes the finite algebra example under the implemented definitions without relying on the uncompiled comparator theorem.

**What the search can establish.** A checked witness is a positive example on its stated carrier. Exhausting every candidate on all smaller positive carrier sizes, with sound pruning, establishes minimum carrier size for the specified witness problem. A failed search through size N establishes only that no witness was found in that fully enumerated range. A timeout or incomplete enumeration cannot establish even that negative bounded result. Neither small positive examples nor bounded failures validate a general undecidability theorem, a universal representation theorem, or a claim that a particular lattice has no finite representation at any size.

This is also historically an area where computation has helped construct examples. DeMeo's 2012 thesis describes searches with GAP and UACalc and reported representations for all lattices of at most seven elements except one possible exception at that time. This is historical context, not a statement that the exception remains unresolved today. [DeMeo, *Congruence lattices of finite algebras*, 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1204.4305).

The older subgroup-interval connection has a quantifier distinction worth preserving. Pálfy and Pudlák's Theorem 2 equates the assertion that **every** finite lattice is a congruence lattice of a finite algebra with the assertion that **every** finite lattice is an interval in a finite group's subgroup lattice. It should not be silently restated as a pointwise equivalence for each selected lattice. [Pálfy and Pudlák, 1980, Theorem 2](https://math.hawaii.edu/~ralph/Classes/619/PalfyPudlak.pdf).

The corpus's September 2026 [preprint](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/preprints/Finite-Congruence-Lattices-Characterization-and-Undecidability-September-24-2026/paper.pdf) makes stronger claims, including a negative answer and undecidability. Its abstract and introduction were inspected for context; its long proof was not audited here. The corpus's [scope document](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/1581d4cbdd69a0faf34b2089614fe2a814316a6a/lean/docs/206.md) explicitly excludes those conclusions from the selected graph-criterion formalization. Our computation therefore takes no position on those stronger claims. Historical sources describing an open problem cannot, by themselves, decide its status in October 2026.

**Elementary carrier lower bound, derived here for the experiment.** Suppose the first four graph conditions hold. Each theta_a is reflexive and symmetric. If x theta_a y and y theta_a z, the triangle inequality gives color(x,z) <= a join a = a, so theta_a is transitive. Thus theta_a is an equivalence relation. If a <= b then theta_a is contained in theta_b. Conversely, if a is not <= b, the separation condition supplies a pair in theta_a but outside theta_b. Hence a maps to theta_a by an order embedding into the equivalence relations on n points.

Now let a_0 < a_1 < ... < a_h be a chain in L, with h strict steps. The corresponding equivalence relations form a strictly increasing chain. Every strict increase merges at least two blocks, reducing the number of blocks by at least one. There are at most n blocks initially and at least one finally, so h <= n-1, or **n >= h+1**. This elementary derivation is ours for documentation; it is not presented as a new mathematical result. It does not depend on the fifth, seed-connectivity condition. The embedding also gives the weaker or complementary counting bound |L| <= Bell(n).

The independent `verify_algebra.py` run passed all seven saved certificates. It imports neither the search nor the seed verifier. It enumerated every total unary function and every partition for each carrier, checked that the color-decreasing functions equal the threshold-preserving functions, and checked that the preserved partitions are exactly the threshold partitions with the stated order. The complete operation tables and partitions are saved in `evidence/verified-algebras.json`, together with input SHA-256 hashes. Its controls include known Bell-number counts, a positive C3 example, and rejection of a missing separating color, an incorrect join table, and a nontransitive relation.

Each carrier size meets the height lower bound. Subject to correctness of the finite computation, these explicit unary algebras therefore attain minimum carrier size for their respective lattices. This conclusion also follows directly for algebras: their congruence relations are equivalence relations on the carrier, so the same block-count argument applies. This is an elementary size argument attached to reproducible examples; we do not claim that these minima were previously unknown. Run `python verify_algebra.py` from this directory to regenerate the independent verification file.

**Useful next direction.** B2, M3, and N5 exercise incomparable colors and joins that chain examples do not exercise. After finding and independently checking witnesses, retain counterexamples that pass the first four conditions but fail seed closure; these are useful regression cases for the difference between an embedding and a full congruence representation. Then optimize the operation list on each fixed carrier: discard unary maps only when exhaustive partition checking still yields exactly the intended congruences. A short list of operation tables and the complete congruence list make a compact, inspectable certificate. The measurable outputs are carrier size, operation count, rejected candidates, runtime, and independently verified congruences. Such results improve the experimental tool and can motivate later conjectures; a novelty claim would require a separate literature comparison and proof.

**Operation minimization completed.** The next bounded experiment above has now been run on all seven saved certificates. `minimize_operations.py` reads the certificate files directly, validates their lattice tables and threshold order embeddings, and enumerates every unary map preserving those exact relations. It then finds the smallest subset with exactly those congruences. `evidence/minimized-algebras.json` contains all admissible operation tables, target and unwanted partitions, chosen operations, optimum values, per-certificate checks, certificate SHA-256 hashes, and the minimizer's SHA-256 hash. The command accepts explicit certificate paths followed by `--out OUTPUT_JSON`; when paths are supplied, only those files are processed.

A tuple lists f(0), f(1), ..., f(n-1), using the saved certificate's vertex labels. Zero operations are permitted by the corpus's `Algebra` definition. On two vertices every partition is a member of C2; on three vertices every partition is a member of M3. Those empty-signature algebras therefore need no operation to exclude an unwanted partition. If a different convention required a nonempty signature, an identity map could be added without changing congruences, and those two counts would become one under that convention.

The optimization is exactly a set-cover problem. Let E be the saved family of threshold partitions and P all partitions of its carrier. Any basic operation in a unary algebra whose congruences are exactly E must preserve each member of E, so it is in the fully enumerated pool End(E). For each such map f, let D_f be the members of P minus E that f fails to preserve. A subset F of the pool has congruences exactly E if and only if the union of its D_f sets is P minus E. Consequently a minimum cover is a minimum list of basic unary operations for this concrete representation. Maps with identical D_f are interchangeable for this objective, and maps with empty D_f cannot help a minimum cover.

The primary optimizer uses 0/1 dynamic programming over coverage bit masks. A separate check re-enumerates the functions, tests preservation using related vertex pairs rather than source-block image consistency, and reconstructs destruction sets with ordinary sets. It enumerates subsets in increasing cardinality and exhausts every smaller cardinality before accepting a cover. The two operation pools, destruction families, and optimum values agreed for every certificate. A final full partition enumeration confirmed each chosen operation list has exactly the target congruences. The record includes the subsets examined at each cardinality. Positive controls cover Bell counts and known cover optima zero, one, and two; negative controls cover an impossible cover, duplicate target relations, a target family missing equality, and removing the unique C3 operation, which introduces two extra congruences.

These are exact minima for the saved carriers and their particular threshold embeddings, subject to the finite program's correctness. They do not establish a minimum operation count over all isomorphic embeddings, larger carriers, or signatures containing operations of higher arity. The independent enumeration is a computational cross-check, not a proof assistant verification or a novelty claim. The compact B3 and N5 algebras are useful examples for inspecting which maps remove which unwanted equivalence relations. A further bounded study could compare all witness embeddings on the same minimum carrier and ask whether their minimum unary-operation counts differ; that question requires an explicit enumeration of those embeddings and is not answered by the present seven certificates.

Review follow-up added a strict four-vertex limit to operation minimization, distinct-string validation for lattice element labels, and a guard in both standalone algebra scripts against an output path resolving to an input certificate. Both overwrite guards were exercised on a disposable copy, which remained byte-identical. Additional CLI checks confirmed that an explicit single certificate processes only that certificate and that a corrupted join table produces a failed result and nonzero exit. All seven final operation pools and recovered congruence families also matched the separately generated `verified-algebras.json` record. The smaller partition enumerator still tests Bell counts through six vertices; this does not expand the supported optimization search beyond four vertices.
